## 3. Today's flight: what to ask for

Real thermal beats translated, so the main value of new footage is **real thermal of what we fail on**. RGB pairing is for **labels** (`rgb_to_thermal_labels.py`) and for registration, not mainly for the translator.

Priority:
1. **Drone flights, camera looking down at the drone over terrain** (the 07-15 case: dark/low-contrast drone below the horizon). Our weakest case; nearly no real data.
2. **Small/far drones:** the same drone at several ranges (≈100 m → 1 km) so it covers 3–30 px. The size gap is our biggest train/test mismatch.
3. **Terrain-only footage, no drone** (Daniel's landscapes): real thermal backgrounds for pasting dark drones + hard negatives. Include confusers on purpose: birds, vehicles, people, warm rocks/roofs, power lines.
4. **Different times of day** (morning/noon/late afternoon): thermal contrast and polarity change with sun and temperature.
5. **Looking up at sky (down-up):** lowest priority, we already have plenty of real sky. Exception: drone crossings sky → horizon → terrain are valuable.

Make it usable:
- RGB + thermal on the **same rig, both started together**, no zoom changes. A sync event at the start: a quick pan or a hand wave in view of both.
- 10–20 s at the start looking at structured scenery (buildings, roads) for registration.
- **Raw/radiometric thermal if the camera can record it** (removes the per-frame AGC stretch we keep fighting). Otherwise note the AGC mode and keep it fixed.
- No burned-in OSD overlays; constant frame rate; high bitrate; original files, not screen recordings.
- A log per clip: time, camera pitch, scenario, drone type; the target drone's flight log (GPS + time) if easy.
- **Eval hygiene:** pick one new terrain/dark session as a held-out test **before** training on any of it.

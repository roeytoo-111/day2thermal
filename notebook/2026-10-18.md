## 3. Failure modes (day camera), what the errors actually are (`detection/src/eval/failure_modes_day.py`, `results/failmodes_0858/`)

**08_58_15 empty-frame false alarms are mostly NOT false.** 15 episodes where a model fires ≥ 0.25 on a frame the GT calls empty:
- Most are real aircraft in the picture: the drone low over the sea at t=3936 (0.57–0.75), a drone/wing on the runway edge, a wing on a wall, a landing/launch pad object. Only a few are genuine: grass, a pylon, a car on a road (t=4060), rubble.
- Of 12 "false positives on drone frames" (a uav box away from the labelled drone), several are the drone itself or a second aircraft: a flying wing at t=3884 at 0.7+ with a clearly visible airframe, wings on the ground, plus a few real false alarms (a car).
- So the label rule "only the airborne drone is a drone" turns real drones into false fires and inflates FF for the more sensitive models. Mean recall at matched false fire (the mean over FF 1–25%) is less affected, absolute FF is not trustworthy.

**Misses (6 episodes, all models):** a dark drone over grey sea/haze at confidence 0.13–0.15, just below the 0.15 threshold. The same hard case for every model: low contrast, small.

**Unseen set "background→uav" (14–16 boxes):** mostly real aircraft not labelled uav: drones on display stands and in a hangar, a model plane held by a person, a drone on a stand, with GT boxes that are loose or the wrong class. It is label ambiguity in the Roboflow-type images, not cloud false alarms.

**flight1014 (10-14 flights):** of 49 false uav boxes, 30 are **distant manned aircraft that GT calls airplane**, predicted uav (airplane AP50 is 0 for every model except v5, 0.63). The rest are terrain: pylon, rubble, pad. No cloud false alarm in the top 32.

**Clouds on Open Images sky** (the earlier concern): fw4 fires on 7.5% of 4,232 sky/cloud images, but the most confident are unlabelled birds, kites and aircraft; the real false alarms are low-confidence (wires, cloud edges, sea, cliffs).

## 6. Thermal pastes in every shade over terrain (N2D), pre-registered

### Measurement that motivates it
- **No dark drones in thermal training.** Not one uav box in `thermal-7-N2-u0` train is darker than its surroundings: 0% dark polarity in every source (s0623, s0623a, s0715, s0715r, Boson, public, pastes). Measure: 5th percentile under the box vs ring median, against 95th percentile vs ring median.
- **The 0715_val test drones are mostly dark:** 15 of 24 sampled boxes are dark polarity, black airframes on bright warm terrain seen from above. That is the look of Daniel's end goal (aircraft below the camera), and every thermal model scores 13–21% mean recall there.
- **The earlier pastes never covered it:** bright sprites on smooth sky only (synth_copy_paste v2 rules), and dark-polarity boxes were explicitly excluded from the sprite pool.

### Dataset (`src/data/paste_terrain.py` → `data/thermal-9-N2D-u0` on the VM)
- **Base:** N2 hard-linked, plus 800 new train images.
- **Shapes:** 2,900 real bright sprites from non-pasted boxes, used as shapes. The shade is set at paste time: 405 dark (−40 to −130 grey levels), 160 grey (±15 to 40), 235 white (+40 to +150).
- **Placement:** on textured windows (std ≥ 10; median 29) of real-session frames, warm enough for dark (median level ≥ 70) or cool enough for white (≤ 200), 2 sizes away from labelled boxes. The change must be visible: ≥ max(15, 1.5 × local noise).
- **Sizes:** 8–45 px, median 19.
- **Visual check:** dark ones read as black silhouettes on terrain. A few white sprites are blob-like or keep a square edge from an imperfect source box.

### Runs (`scripts/night_main12.sh`)
bosonN2D_s0 and s1: N2's recipe, yolo26s-p2, rgbcurrent init, so the pastes are the only change against bosonN2_s0 / s1.

### Reading
Same rule as §5: seed-mean difference beyond the larger seed range.
- **Primary:** 0715_val (dark over terrain) and Arsuf R10 ≥ 25 px.
- **Guard:** Boson 08_58_15 / 10_15_59, Arsuf mean recall and the 0708/0730 sets must not drop beyond spread. The danger is new false fires on terrain, the reason N2 halved the sky pastes.

## 9. Working protocol from now on (Daniel, 10-18)

**1. One case matrix, both cameras** (`src/eval/case_matrix.py`).
- Every labelled drone frame is tagged from the pixels: shade in each camera (black / grey / white against its ring), background from the day frame (smooth sky / cloud and edges / ground), size.
- Each case is scored at the pipeline's operating points (thermal 0.40, day 0.40): thermal recall, day recall, blend recall, and the overlap (both / thermal only / day only / neither), plus the false fire of each camera and of the blend.

**2. The Boson camera decides.**
- Deployed-camera sets: 08_58_15 (day + thermal GT, corrected v2 after the episode review), 10_15_59 (thermal), and future Boson flights.
- Guards: the old-camera sets (Arsuf, 07-02 / 07-15 / 07-30) and the RGB test sets. A change must not drop on them beyond spread, but it is chosen on Boson.

**3. Models are judged in pairs.**
- A thermal model is worth its cost if it adds found drones where the day model misses ("thermal only" on the cases the day camera is weak at, e.g. low contrast in day, smooth sky), not if it duplicates the day detections.
- The same holds for an RGB change.

**4. Bottlenecks drive the data work.**
- The "neither" cell per case, weighted by how often the case occurs, ranks what to fix.
- Each fix is one change, pre-registered, 2 seeds, scored on the same matrix.

**5. The real pipeline confirms.**
- Offline numbers rank models (the day evaluation is an upper bound: it tiles the whole 4K frame, while the pipeline sweeps 2 × 2 tiles at about 25% coverage per frame).
- Finalists are replayed through the interceptor with `-ev` (Daniel, on the Jetson) and scored with `tools/pipeline_eval`.

**6. The biggest limit is Boson ground truth.**
- 08_58_15 gives 59 drone frames with both cameras, after the day-detection time match.
- One more labelled Boson session with both cameras (ideally with the drone low over ground) would do more for every decision than any single training run.
